# AP Japanese Studio Search & Discovery Playbook

## Goal

Help students, families, and educators find AP Japanese Studio through Patreon discovery and ordinary search engines, then understand the page quickly enough to choose a useful free or paid starting point.

Search visibility is not guaranteed. The goal is accurate relevance, clear structure, useful public content, and consistent language—not keyword stuffing.

## Official Patreon findings

Patreon currently states that:

- Explore categorization uses content type, tagline, and description.
- A clear tagline and About section improve the chance that the right fans understand and discover a creator.
- Public or free content gives potential members a useful sample.
- The tagline remains part of search and discovery; the first lines of About are prominent on the public page.
- Tags should represent recurring topics and words that fans may search for.
- Google-facing SEO benefits from a consistent page name, a short relevant URL, readable headings, relevant content, and links from reputable sites.

## Search audiences

### Students

Common needs:

- understand the AP Japanese exam
- practice speaking, reading, listening, and writing
- learn how to respond under time pressure
- reduce anxiety about unfamiliar prompts
- find approachable examples in English and やさしい日本語

### Teachers

Common needs:

- AP Japanese lesson ideas
- classroom-ready practice routines
- scaffolds for mixed proficiency levels
- culturally responsible discussion prompts
- efficient formative assessment and feedback ideas

### Families and counselors

Common needs:

- understand what AP Japanese involves
- learn how the course and exam are structured
- find trustworthy study support

Do not combine every audience in every article. Choose one primary search intent per post.

## Post title rules

A searchable title should contain:

1. the topic people search for
2. the specific benefit or task
3. the series label only when it helps navigation

Good patterns:

- AP Japanese Speaking Practice: Tell Your Japan Story
- AP Japanese 2027 Exam Guide: What Changed and How to Prepare
- AP Japanese Cultural Comparison: A Simple Three-Step Practice
- AP Japanese Lesson Ideas: Low-Pressure Speaking Routines

Avoid:

- titles that contain only a clever series name
- repeated exclamation marks
- vague promises such as Master AP Japanese Fast
- putting AP Japanese at the front of every sentence in the body
- changing a published URL or title solely to chase a temporary keyword

## Opening paragraph rule

Within the first 80 words:

- name the exact learner or teacher problem
- use the primary search phrase once, naturally
- explain what the reader will be able to do
- acknowledge one realistic worry

Example:

AP Japanese speaking practice can feel difficult when students know the vocabulary but freeze as soon as the timer starts. In this activity, we slow the task down, connect one personal example to one cultural idea, and build a response step by step. It does not have to sound perfect on the first tryですよ。

## Headings and readability

- Use short Heading 3 subheads in Patreon articles.
- Keep one clear idea per section.
- Use bullets for steps, choices, and checklists.
- Add breathing room before every subhead.
- Write for humans first; do not repeat keywords unnaturally.
- Make English and やさしい日本語 sections easy to distinguish.
- Keep the established warm, specific, classroom-aware voice.

## Free-content discovery path

Publish enough free content that a new visitor can confirm the quality before joining.

Recommended repeating cycle:

1. Free explainer answering a real search question
2. Free mini-practice that can be completed in 5–10 minutes
3. Paid guided practice with scaffolds and model thinking
4. Educator adaptation or classroom toolkit

Useful free search topics:

- What changed in the AP Japanese exam?
- What is on the AP Japanese exam?
- How can I practice AP Japanese speaking?
- A five-minute AP Japanese cultural comparison warm-up
- Three mistakes students worry about—and what to do next
- やさしい日本語でわかる AP Japanese

A free article must be genuinely useful. Do not make the preview feel like an empty advertisement.

## Authority and external links

- Cite public primary sources such as College Board for exam facts.
- Link to the Patreon page from owned public profiles when the founder approves.
- Do not copy official exam questions, rubrics, or third-party worksheets.
- Do not place private Drive, Docs, Canva, preview, or edit links in public metadata.
- Do not claim College Board endorsement or affiliation.

## Sustainable publishing rhythm

Quality and consistency matter more than volume.

Suggested minimum:

- one useful free post every two weeks
- one deeper member post connected to that free topic
- one educator-focused post or adaptation each month
- refresh one older public post each month when facts or links change

This is a planning baseline, not a promise to members.

## What to measure

Record monthly, without personal member data:

- public post views
- page visits
- free joins
- paid conversions
- top entry posts
- search phrases visible in approved analytics tools
- collection and tag engagement

Do not store names, emails, student information, or raw private analytics exports in this public repository.

## Founder approval gates

Ask before:

- changing the Patreon URL
- changing page name, tagline, or About text
- publishing, updating, or saving Patreon content
- adding a public link from another account
- creating a collaboration post
- making claims about exam changes that have not been verified against current primary sources
